No Identity, No Interop

Every new API, every new connection, and every new partner
requires a trust conversation. The volume and methods of
connection have expanded but how we establish trust with the
org on the other end hasn’t changed.


The industry has made incredible progress in the realm of
individual ID.


Organizational ID hasn’t kept pace.


Orgs still manually onboard newcomers, building trust from
scratch every new connection. And those newcomers have to
run through the same hoops time and time again.


The result?

  • Onboarding takes weeks

  • Trust decisions rely on spreadsheets, contracts, and manual
    human review


As connectivity grows, manual trust across networks just isn’t
scalable.

Already, healthcare exchanges data billions of times each year. As FHIR-
based APIs come online, that rate of connection with continue to grow.

We cannot continue to rely on manual, duplicative onboarding that is slow,
expensive, and fragile.


As an industry we’ve answered the question, “can we connect?” We can.


But we are still asking, “Can we trust this connection?”


The bottleneck has become urgent:

  • CMS-0057-F: By January 1, 2027, mandatory API readiness for payer-
    to-payer exchange goes into effect (among other types).

  • Healthcare Provider Directories: Directories independently collect
    organizational data, resulting in duplication and inaccuracies.

  • Inadequate ID: Existing identifiers (NPIs, TINs, and standard digital
    certificates all have serious limitations and were not designed to serve
    as universal organizational identifiers.

Healthcare isn’t the first industry to face the organizational ID problem.
Following the 2008 financial crisis, the G20, established the Global Legal Entity
Identifier Foundation (GLEIF), to create the LEI, a globally unique 20-character
code that identifies legal organizations.


Today, over 2.6 million entities in more than 200 countries hold LEIs, including
many US healthcare organizations. Now, the open-source, ISO-standardized
Verifiable LEI (vLEI) brings organizational identity into the digital realm.

The Solution:

Importing a Proven, Global Standard

WHAT IS THE vLEI?

In practice, the vLEI proves who can do what on behalf of which organizations across large-scale networks, without requiring
manual, human effort. The vLEI is a cryptographically verifiable credential that acts as a tamper-proof digital assertion. It proves
instantly and automatically:

WHO an organization is

WHO within that organization is authorized to act on its behalf

WHAT purposes of use they are authorized to assert.
